Projectile Video Project Deliverables & Student Directions

Three App Steps: Track the Flight, Explain the Motion, and Submit

Step 1: Track the Flight (Student Directions 1–3)

1. Record the ball in flight: Use a stationary camera perpendicular to the motion plane. Place a known length in the same plane as the ball. Capture ascent and descent after release and before contact. Keep the ball visible; avoid camera movement and zoom. Save the original video and confirm its actual frame rate, especially for slow motion.
2. Set the scale and origin: Use meters and seconds. Choose right and up as positive. Put \(y = 0\) at a visible ground reference and place \(x = 0\) below the release point. Set \(t = 0\) at the first tracked airborne frame; “initial” quantities refer to that frame. Record the initial height \(y_0\), frame rate, and calibration length. The clip need not end on the ground.
3. Track and make a prediction: Track the ball’s center at about 10–15 instants across ascent and descent. Include points near the highest point. Save one screenshot showing calibration and tracking. Before revealing generated velocity and acceleration plots, sketch/select your predicted shapes; save the attempt for comparison.

Step 2: Explain the Motion (Student Directions 4–5)

4. Build one panel with six graphs: Use the same time interval on every graph. Label axes and units, show a readable numerical scale and zero, and distinguish tracked points from fitted or derived curves. Arrange the plots in two columns (Horizontal motion vs. Vertical motion). Record the fitted equations with units:
  • For \(x = mt + c\), \(v_x = m\) and \(a_x = 0\text{ m/s}^2\). A zero \(a_x\) plot follows from the chosen linear model, not a separate measurement.
  • For \(y = At^2 + Bt + C\), \(v_y = 2At + B\) and \(a_y = 2A\). \(C\) is the fitted initial height (may differ slightly from first tracked height \(y_0\)); \(B\) is the fitted initial vertical velocity.
5. Calculate and compare (using \(a_{y,\text{ref}} = -9.8\text{ m/s}^2\)): Define \(t_{\text{hang}}\) (first to last tracked frame) and \(t_{\text{up}}\) (observed time from first tracked frame to apex, read from video).
  • 5a. Horizontal velocity: Show one slope calculation on the fitted \(x\)–\(t\) line: \(v_{0x} = \Delta x/\Delta t\). Explain why it is constant in your model.
  • 5b. Vertical acceleration: Show one slope calculation on the derived \(v_y\)–\(t\) line: \(a_{y,\text{fit}} = \Delta v_y/\Delta t\). Compare with \(-9.8\text{ m/s}^2\).
  • 5c. Initial vertical velocity: Estimate \(v_{0y,\text{pred}} = -a_{y,\text{ref}} t_{\text{up}}\). Compare with \(B\) from your position fit and explain a possible source of disagreement.
  • 5d. Displacements and final position: Predict \(\Delta x = v_{0x}t_{\text{hang}}\) and \(\Delta y = v_{0y,\text{pred}}t_{\text{hang}} + \frac{1}{2}a_{y,\text{ref}}t_{\text{hang}}^2\). Compare with tracked endpoint changes, then predict final height \(y_0 + \Delta y\).
  • 5e. Maximum height: Predict upward displacement using \(t_{\text{up}}\) (not \(t_{\text{hang}}\)): \(\Delta y_{\text{up}} = v_{0y,\text{pred}}t_{\text{up}} + \frac{1}{2}a_{y,\text{ref}}t_{\text{up}}^2\). Add \(y_0\) and compare with highest tracked position.
  • 5f. Initial speed and angle: Calculate \(v_0 = \sqrt{v_{0x}^2 + v_{0y,\text{pred}}^2}\) and \(\theta = \tan^{-1}(v_{0y,\text{pred}}/v_{0x})\).
Connect the graphs using signed area & Explain the motion: Split the fitted \(v_y\)–\(t\) graph at its zero crossing, sum the positive ascent area and negative descent area, add fitted initial height \(C\), and compare with fitted \(y(t_{\text{hang}})\). Identify upward/downward intervals and \(v_y = 0\), explain whether the ball is at rest then, explain the negative sign of \(a_y\) on both sides of the apex, and state one specific measurement limitation tied to a result.

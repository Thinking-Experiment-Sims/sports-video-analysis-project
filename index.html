Physics of Sports Analytics: Complete Project Guide

Video Analysis Lab Rubric, Kinematic Formulas & Step-by-Step Instructions

🎯 Project Objective & Workflow

In this laboratory project, you act as a sports analytics biomechanist analyzing the two-dimensional projectile motion of a sports ball. You will track the projectile frame-by-frame, construct 6 kinematics graphs, use the tangent slope method to construct vertical velocity, verify theoretical kinematic models with percent differences, and export an 8-slide presentation deck.

The 5 Project Stages:
  1. Step 1: Video & Scale — Calibrate the coordinate origin \((0, 0)\) and real-world meter scale stick.
  2. Step 2: Track Motion — Step through frames and mark the projectile's center of mass with circular reticles.
  3. Step 3: 6-Graph Studio & Tangents — Inspect the 6 kinematics curves and sample 10 tangent slopes along \(y(t)\) to construct \(v_y(t)\).
  4. Step 4: Guided Calculations — Compute launch speed \(v_0\), release angle \(\theta\), \(y_{\max}\), \(t_{\text{hang}}\), \(\Delta x\), and percent differences. Write a 5-sentence reflection.
  5. Step 5: Presentation & Reports — Generate an 8-slide PowerPoint (.pptx) deck matching classroom grading rubrics.

📏 Step 1: Setting Scale & Coordinate Origin

Accurate kinematics depends entirely on correct spatial calibration.

  • Setting the Coordinate Origin (0, 0): Click Set Origin in the toolbar. Click anywhere on the video (typically the launch point or ground level directly below release) to place the axes. You can drag the origin point anytime.
  • Setting the Scale Stick: Click Set Scale. Click and drag across a known reference length in the frame (such as the 1.00 m reference stick on the floor, a meter stick, or athlete's height).
  • Calibrate Input: In the right sidebar under Scale & Calibration, enter the known length in meters (e.g. 1.00) and click Calibrate.

🎯 Step 2: Frame-by-Frame Motion Tracking

Track the projectile throughout its airborne flight (from release until right before impact).

  • Select Tool: Click Add Points in the toolbar.
  • Reticle Placement: Place the crosshair/circle directly at the center of the ball on each frame.
  • Auto-Advancing: By default, each click advances the video by 2 frames. You can change this to 1 frame using the Step dropdown.
  • Time Zero (\(t_0\)): After tracking, click Set \(t_0\) at First Pt so that the release frame corresponds to \(t = 0.000\text{ s}\).

📈 Step 3: The 6 Kinematics Graphs & Tangent Slope Method

Why does the rubric ask for 10 tangent slopes on the vertical position curve?

The Physics of Tangent Lines:

By definition, instantaneous velocity is the derivative (slope) of the position-time curve: \[v_y(t) = \frac{dy}{dt}\] When you fit a quadratic curve \(y(t) = At^2 + Bt + C\) to your vertical position data, the tangent line touching the curve at time \(t\) has slope \(m = 2At + B\).

By sampling 10 points along the trajectory, recording their tangent slopes, and plotting those slopes versus time on Graph 3, you construct the vertical velocity graph \(v_y(t)\)!

The linear fit to these 10 tangent velocity points yields: \[v_y(t) = a_y t + v_{0y}\] The slope of this line directly reveals the vertical acceleration of gravity \(a_y\) (theoretically \(-9.80\text{ m/s}^2\)), while the y-intercept reveals the initial vertical speed \(v_{0y}\).

The 6 Required Project Graphs:

  1. Graph 1: Vertical Position \(y(t)\) — Parabolic curve of best fit \(y(t) = \frac{1}{2}a_y t^2 + v_{0y}t + y_0\).
  2. Graph 2: Horizontal Position \(x(t)\) — Linear fit \(x(t) = v_{0x}t + x_0\) (slope = constant horizontal velocity).
  3. Graph 3: Vertical Velocity \(v_y(t)\) — Linear fit through the 10 tangent slopes (slope = \(a_y\)).
  4. Graph 4: Horizontal Velocity \(v_x(t)\) — Linear fit showing zero acceleration (\(a_x \approx 0\)).
  5. Graph 5: Vertical Acceleration \(a_y(t)\) — Horizontal constant line at \(a_y\) compared against standard \(g = -9.80\text{ m/s}^2\).
  6. Graph 6: Horizontal Acceleration \(a_x(t)\) — Horizontal constant line at \(a_x \approx 0.00\text{ m/s}^2\).

🧮 Step 4: Theoretical Calculations & Percent Differences

Using the coefficients from your curve fits, verify the theoretical models:

  • Initial Speed: \(v_0 = \sqrt{v_{0x}^2 + v_{0y}^2}\)
  • Launch Angle: \(\theta = \arctan\left(\frac{v_{0y}}{v_{0x}}\right)\)
  • Calculated Hang Time: \(t_{\text{hang}} = \frac{-v_{0y} - \sqrt{v_{0y}^2 - 2a_y(y_{\text{final}} - y_0)}}{a_y}\)
  • Calculated Range: \(\Delta x = v_{0x} \cdot t_{\text{hang}}\)
  • Calculated Max Height: \(y_{\max} = y_0 - \frac{v_{0y}^2}{2a_y}\)
  • Percent Difference Formula: \[\text{\% Diff} = \frac{|\text{Measured} - \text{Calculated}|}{\frac{\text{Measured} + \text{Calculated}}{2}} \times 100\%\]

1. Values from your best-fit lines and curves

\(x(t) = mt + c\) gives \(x_0 = c\), \(v_{0x} = m\), \(a_x = 0\). \(y(t) = At^2 + Bt + C\) gives \(y_0 = C\), \(v_{0y} = B\), \(a_y = 2A\).

2. Hang time

Calculate: solve \(y_{\text{final}} = y_0 + v_{0y}t + \tfrac{1}{2}a_y t^2\) for \(t\) (quadratic formula), where \(y_{\text{final}}\) is the height of your last tracked point. Measure: last tracked time − first tracked time.

3. Range (horizontal displacement)

Calculate: \(\Delta x = v_{0x}t + \tfrac{1}{2}a_x t^2\) using your calculated hang time. Measure: \(x_{\text{final}} - x_{\text{initial}}\) from your tracked data.

4. Maximum height (above the ground)

Calculate: at the top \(v_y = 0\), so \(0 = v_{0y}^2 + 2a_y\Delta y_{\text{up}}\); then \(y_{\max} = y_0 + \Delta y_{\text{up}}\). Measure: the highest point in your data table.

5. Launch speed   •   6. Launch angle

\(v_0 = \sqrt{v_{0x}^2 + v_{0y}^2}\) and \(\theta = \tan^{-1}(v_{0y} / v_{0x})\) above the horizontal (calculator in degrees).

7. Consistency check: initial velocity from your measured values

Use your measured range, hang time and maximum height (not the fit values) with the equations for accelerating motion: \(v_{0x} = \Delta x_{\text{meas}} / t_{\text{meas}}\) and \(v_{0y} = \sqrt{2|a_y|\,(y_{\max,\text{meas}} - y_0)}\). Compare with \(v_{0x}\) and \(v_{0y}\) from Calculation 1.

💡 Concept Hint: How does area on a velocity graph connect to displacement?
• The area between a velocity graph and the time axis represents displacement.
• With a constant acceleration, each region forms a geometric triangle.
• Areas above the time axis (\(v_y > 0\)) are positive; areas below (\(v_y < 0\)) are negative.
• Adding net displacement \(\Delta y_{\text{area}}\) to your fitted initial height \(C\) should match \(y_{\text{fit}}(t_{\text{hang}})\).

💡 Examples of video limitations
• Frame spacing at the apex: True peak occurs between video frames, making highest tracked \(y\) slightly lower than \(y_{\max}\).
• Motion blur / clicking center: Fast motion blurs the ball, adding small scatter to slope \(v_{0x}\) or \(a_{y,\text{fit}}\).
• Camera perspective / scale plane: If the ball moves slightly closer/farther than the scale bar, pixel-to-meter conversion shifts slightly.

Quick Student Guide & Rubric

Tabs 1–3: Track, Predict & Build the 6 Graphs

  • Tab 1 (Track Video): Calibrate a known length in the motion plane, place \(y=0\) at the floor and \(x=0\) below release, track 10–15 airborne points across the apex, and record observed rise time \(t_{\text{up}}\).
  • Tab 2 (Predict): Inspect \(x(t)\) and \(y(t)\) and lock your predicted shapes for \(v_x, v_y, a_x, a_y\).
  • Tab 3 (Build Graphs):
    • 3A: Select the Linear fit for \(x(t)\) and Quadratic fit for \(y(t)\).
    • 3B: Measure tangent slopes on \(y(t)\) across ascent, apex, and descent to plot points on \(v_y(t)\), then select the Linear fit for \(v_y(t)\) and Constant fit for \(v_x(t)\).
    • 3C–3D: Select the fits for \(a_x(t)\) and \(a_y(t)\), then review the complete 6-graph panel.

Tabs 4–5: Projectile Calculations (5a–5f) & Signed Area

  • 4A (Slopes 5a–5b): Compute \(v_{0x} = \Delta x / \Delta t\) on fitted \(x(t)\) and \(a_{y,\text{fit}} = \Delta v_y / \Delta t\) on fitted \(v_y(t)\).
  • 4B (5c–5d): Using \(a_{y,\text{ref}} = -9.8\text{ m/s}^2\) and video \(t_{\text{up}}\), find \(v_{0y,\text{pred}} = -a_{y,\text{ref}}t_{\text{up}}\), then predict \(\Delta x, \Delta y,\) and \(y_{\text{final}} = y_0 + \Delta y\) at \(t_{\text{hang}}\).
  • 4C (5e–5f): Find \(y_{\max} = y_0 + \Delta y_{\text{up}}\) using \(t_{\text{up}}\) (not \(t_{\text{hang}}\)), plus launch speed \(v_0\) and angle \(\theta\).
  • 5A–5B (Area & Motion): Sum the positive ascent area and negative descent area on \(v_y(t)\), add fitted \(C\) to check \(y_{\text{fit}}(t_{\text{hang}})\), and explain the intervals, apex motion, and one limitation.

Tabs 4–5: Calculations 1–7, Apex & Conclusion

  • 1 (Fit values): Read \(x_0, y_0, v_{0x}, v_{0y}, a_x, a_y\) from your fits (\(v_{0x} = m\), \(v_{0y} = B\), \(a_y = 2A\)); compare \(a_y\) with \(-9.8\text{ m/s}^2\).
  • 2–4 (Hang time, range, maximum height): Calculate each with the equations for accelerating motion, measure it from your tracked data, and find the percent difference.
  • 5–6 (Launch speed and angle): \(v_0 = \sqrt{v_{0x}^2 + v_{0y}^2}\), \(\theta = \tan^{-1}(v_{0y}/v_{0x})\).
  • 7 (Consistency check): Find \(v_{0x}\) and \(v_{0y}\) again from your measured range, hang time and maximum height, and compare with Calculation 1.
  • Tab 5B (Motion Meaning & Conclusion): Explain why the ball is not at rest in 2D at \(v_y = 0\), explain negative \(a_y\), state your measurement limitation with direction (too high vs. too low), and write your gravity conclusion.

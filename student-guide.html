Projectile Video Project Deliverables

Goal: Use a video of a ball launched upward and forward to connect position, velocity, acceleration, and projectile calculations. Submit one report containing your setup evidence, one six-graph panel, your calculations, and brief explanations.

App Step 1: Track the Flight (Student Directions 1–3)

Step 1 • Record

1. Record the ball in flight

Use a stationary camera perpendicular to the motion plane. Place a known length in the same plane as the ball. Capture ascent and descent after release and before contact. Keep the ball visible; avoid camera movement and zoom. Save the original video and confirm its actual frame rate, especially for slow motion. (If using a built-in practice clip, note that synthetic clips are labeled as models.)

Step 1 • Calibrate

2. Set the scale and origin

Use meters and seconds. Choose right (\(+x\)) and up (\(+y\)) as positive. Put \(y = 0\) at a visible ground reference and place \(x = 0\) below the release point. Set \(t = 0\) at the first tracked airborne frame; “initial” quantities refer to that frame. Record the initial height \(y_0\), frame rate, and calibration length. The clip need not end on the ground.

Step 1 → Step 2A • Track & Predict

3. Track and make a prediction

Track the ball’s center at about 10–15 instants across ascent and descent. Include points near the highest point. Record the observed rise time \(t_{\text{up}}\) from the first tracked frame to the apex as read from the video, allowing for frame spacing. Save one screenshot showing calibration and tracking. Before revealing generated velocity and acceleration plots in Step 2A, select and describe your predicted shapes; save your first attempt for comparison.

App Step 2: Explain the Motion (Student Directions 4–5)

Step 2B • Graph

4. Build one panel with six graphs

Include horizontal and vertical position, velocity, and acceleration over the same time interval \([0, t_{\text{hang}}]\). Arrange horizontal graphs in the left column and vertical graphs in the right column, with rows for position, velocity, and acceleration. Label axes with quantities and SI units, include numerical scales, and mark zero. Show tracked points and fitted curves clearly.

Fit \(x = mt + c\) and \(y = At^2 + Bt + C\) once, then derive \(v_x = m\), \(a_x = 0\), \(v_y = 2At + B\), and \(a_y = 2A\) from those fits. Record the fitted equations with units. Note that \(C\) is the fitted initial height while \(y_0\) is the measured first height, and \(B\) is the fitted initial vertical velocity. Explain how the velocity and acceleration graphs follow from the position graphs, and why \(a_x = 0\) is a consequence of the linear horizontal model rather than a separate measurement.

Step 2C • Calculate & Connect

5. Complete the projectile calculations and graph checks

Define \(t_{\text{hang}}\) as the time from the first to last tracked frame and \(t_{\text{up}}\) as the observed time from the first tracked frame to the apex, read from the video allowing for frame spacing. Do not obtain \(t_{\text{up}}\) from the velocity equation you are checking, and do not assume \(t_{\text{hang}} = 2t_{\text{up}}\) when endpoint heights differ. Show equations, substitutions, signs, and units. Keep measured values, estimates from fits, and model predictions using \(a_{y,\text{ref}} = -9.8\text{ m/s}^2\) clearly labeled:

  • a. Horizontal velocity: Show one slope calculation \(v_{0x} = \Delta x / \Delta t\) on the fitted \(x\)–\(t\) line and identify why it is constant in your model.
  • b. Vertical acceleration: Show one slope calculation \(a_{y,\text{fit}} = \Delta v_y / \Delta t\) using two points on the derived \(v_y\)–\(t\) line and compare it with \(-9.8\text{ m/s}^2\). This checks your interpretation of the fit, not a new measurement.
  • c. Initial vertical velocity: Use \(v_y = 0\) at the apex, \(a_{y,\text{ref}} = -9.8\text{ m/s}^2\), and observed \(t_{\text{up}}\) to estimate \(v_{0y,\text{pred}} = -a_{y,\text{ref}}t_{\text{up}}\). Compare it with \(B\) from your position fit and explain a possible source of disagreement.
  • d. Displacements and final position: Use \(v_{0x}\) and \(v_{0y,\text{pred}}\) with \(a_{y,\text{ref}} = -9.8\text{ m/s}^2\) to predict \(\Delta x = v_{0x}t_{\text{hang}}\) and \(\Delta y = v_{0y,\text{pred}}t_{\text{hang}} + \frac{1}{2}a_{y,\text{ref}}t_{\text{hang}}^2\). Compare with tracked endpoint changes, then add \(y_0\) to predict final height \(y_{\text{final}} = y_0 + \Delta y\).
  • e. Maximum height: Use \(v_{0y,\text{pred}}\), \(a_{y,\text{ref}} = -9.8\text{ m/s}^2\), and \(t_{\text{up}}\) (not \(t_{\text{hang}}\)) to predict upward displacement \(\Delta y_{\text{up}}\). Add \(y_0\) to get \(y_{\max}\) and compare with the highest tracked position, allowing for frame spacing.
  • f. Initial speed and angle: Use \(v_{0x}\) and \(v_{0y,\text{pred}}\) to calculate \(v_0 = \sqrt{v_{0x}^2 + v_{0y,\text{pred}}^2}\) and \(\theta = \tan^{-1}(v_{0y,\text{pred}} / v_{0x})\) above \(+x\) at the first tracked instant.

Connect the graphs using signed area: On the fitted \(v_y\)–\(t\) graph, split \([0, t_{\text{hang}}]\) at its zero crossing \(t_{\text{zero}}\). Show the positive area during ascent and the negative area during descent using triangles or trapezoids, add them to get vertical displacement \(\Delta y_{\text{area}}\), and add the fitted initial height \(C\) to compare with the fitted position at \(t_{\text{hang}}\), \(y_{\text{fit}}(t_{\text{hang}}) = At_{\text{hang}}^2 + Bt_{\text{hang}} + C\).

Explain the motion briefly: Identify when the ball moves upward, when it moves downward (excluding the zero crossing), and the instant \(v_y = 0\). Explain whether the ball is completely at rest then in two dimensions. Identify when vertical acceleration is negative and explain its negative sign on both sides of the apex. State one specific measurement limitation tied to a reported result.

App Step 3: Submit Report & Grading (Student Direction 6)

Step 3 • Submit & Assess

6. Submit one report and prepare for the individual assessment

Submit one report containing your setup evidence, the six-graph panel, your saved predictions and revisions, your calculations, and your explanations. Keep the original video and saved .json progress available; submit them only if requested.

Individual Assessment (37 Points — Separate from Lab Report): In class, after a brief multi-stage cart \(v\)–\(t\) graph review, you will complete a paper assessment on projectile calculations (Part 1: 14 points, using \(g = 10\text{ m/s}^2\) on paper) and multi-stage motion graphs (Part 2: 23 points: accelerations, piecewise \(x\)–\(t\) and \(a\)–\(t\) graphs, signed areas, direction intervals, and velocity-acceleration sign reasoning).

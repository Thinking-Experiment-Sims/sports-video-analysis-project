Student Guide: Physics of Sports Analytics Video Project

Your step-by-step handbook for tracking a projectile in sports, mastering Unit 2 motion graphs (tangent slopes, signed $v$-$t$ area, and physical motion intervals), completing your kinematics derivations without shortcuts, and exporting your 11-slide PowerPoint presentation.

1. What Is This Assignment About?

In this project, you will analyze a real video of a ball in free flight (such as a basketball shot, soccer kick, volleyball serve, or football pass) to test how closely real athletic motion matches our Unit 2 Constant Acceleration & Projectile Motion model.

⚠️ Three Golden Rules for Full Credit:
  1. Show Every Algebraic Step (No Shortcut Formulas!): Start every calculation in Step 4 from our fundamental kinematics equations ($x(t) = x_0 + v_{0x}t$, $y(t) = y_0 + v_{0y}t + \frac{1}{2}a_y t^2$, $v_y(t) = v_{0y} + a_y t$, and $v_{fy}^2 = v_{0y}^2 + 2a_y\Delta y$). Do not use pre-derived shortcut formulas!
  2. Use Two Points on the Tangent Line ($\Delta y / \Delta t$): Never divide $y/t$ at a single point to find instantaneous velocity on a curve! Always use two distinct points $(t_1, y_1)$ and $(t_2, y_2)$ on the tangent line: $v_y = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{t_2 - t_1}$.
  3. Save Your .json Lab File Frequently: Click 💾 Save Lab (.json) in the top bar whenever you pause work so you can reload your session anytime.

2. Practice & Review Simulations (Unit 2 Motion Graphs)

Before or during Step 3, use these 4 interactive simulations to review the exact graphing skills tested in this project:

1. Graph Sprint (x-t ↔ v-t ↔ a-t)

3. Step-by-Step Simulation Walkthrough

Step 1: Video Upload, Origin $(0,0)$ & Scale Calibration

Stage 1
  1. Enter Your Name & Teacher: Type your full name and choose your teacher (Mr. Lopez, Ms. Chen, or Mr. Tipton) at the top of the page.
  2. Open Your Video: Click Open Video to load your sports clip (keep the camera completely still and perpendicular to the motion!), or click Sports Presets.
  3. Set Coordinate Origin $(0,0)$: Click Set Origin and click on the ground directly beneath the ball's release point to place the $+x$ and $+y$ axes.
  4. Calibrate Scale Stick: Click Calibrate Scale, drag the orange bar across an object of known length in the video (e.g., a $1.00\text{ m}$ stick or a player of known height), enter the length in meters, and click Apply.

Step 2: Frame-by-Frame Free-Flight Tracking

Stage 2
  1. Find the Launch Frame: Use the frame-step buttons (|< and >|) or keyboard arrow keys (← / →) to advance to the first frame after the ball leaves the athlete's hand or foot.
  2. Track 10–15 Free-Flight Points: With Track Point selected, click the center of the ball in each frame while it is in the air (stop before it hits the ground, net, or rim!).
  3. Zero the Clock: Click Set $t = 0$ at 1st Pt so your first tracked frame starts at $t = 0.000\text{ s}$.

Step 3: 6-Graph Studio, Two-Point Tangents & Motion Intervals

Stage 3 • Core Graphing Mastery

In Step 3, the computer plots your points, so you are graded on your tangent slope calculations and graph analysis—not on computer plotting:

  • A. Two-Point Tangent Slope Worksheet (On Graph 1 [$y(t)$]): Move the Tangent Slider to choose a time $t$. Read the two points $P_1(t_1, y_1)$ and $P_2(t_2, y_2)$ on the dashed tangent line, type them into the worksheet, and compute the slope on your calculator: \[v_y = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta t} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{t_2 - t_1}\] Click ✓ Verify & Log Two-Point Slope. Once you manually verify 3 two-point tangent slopes, the Quick-Log Remaining Tangent Point button unlocks so you can log the remaining 7 points (10 total) to build Graph 3 [$v_y(t)$].
  • B. Visual Signed-Area Shading (On Graph 3 [$v_y(t)$]): Keep Shade Signed $v$-$t$ Areas ($\Delta y, \Delta x$) checked. Notice how the Teal triangle above the axis represents positive upward displacement ($+\Delta y_{\text{up}} > 0$) and the Amber triangle below the axis represents negative downward displacement ($\Delta y_{\text{down}} < 0$).
  • C. Graph Shape Translation & Physical Motion Intervals: Complete Part A (translating $y(t) \to v_y(t) \to a_y(t)$ and $x(t) \to v_x(t) \to a_x(t)$) and Part B:
    • Stopping ($v_y = 0$): Identify the exact apex time $t_{\text{apex}}$ where $v_y(t)$ crosses zero, and explain why $v_x \neq 0$ at the apex.
    • Direction Intervals: Enter the time interval $[0, t_{\text{apex}}]$ where the ball moves upward ($v_y > 0$) and $[t_{\text{apex}}, t_{\text{end}}]$ where it moves downward ($v_y < 0$).
    • Sign of Acceleration: Enter the interval where $a_y < 0$ (the entire flight $[0, t_{\text{end}}]$!) and explain why acceleration is negative even while the ball is rising.
    Click ✓ Verify Graph Translation & Intervals until both badges turn green!

Step 4: Guided Calculations Workspace (Show Full Work)

Stage 4
  1. Section 1 (Graph Constants): Enter $x_0, v_{0x}, a_x, y_0, v_{0y}, a_y$ from your Step 3 particular equations.
  2. Section 1B (Signed $v$-$t$ Area & Slope):
    • Calculate $a_y = \frac{\Delta v_y}{\Delta t}$ from your $v_y(t)$ line.
    • Calculate the positive ascent triangle area $\Delta y_{\text{up}} = +\frac{1}{2}v_{0y}t_{\text{apex}}$ and negative descent triangle area $\Delta y_{\text{down}} = \frac{1}{2}v_{fy}(t_{\text{end}} - t_{\text{apex}}) < 0$.
    • Find net displacement $\Delta y_{\text{total}} = \Delta y_{\text{up}} + \Delta y_{\text{down}}$, apex height $y_{\text{max}} = y_0 + \Delta y_{\text{up}}$, and final height $y_{\text{final}} = y_0 + \Delta y_{\text{total}}$.
  3. Sections 2–6 (Algebraic Kinematics): Write out your step-by-step algebraic equations and substitutions for Range ($\Delta x$), Hang Time ($t_{\text{hang}}$), Max Height ($y_{\text{max}}$), Launch Speed ($v_0$), and Launch Angle ($\theta$).
  4. Section 7 & 8 (Comparison Table & Reflection): Click ✓ Check & Verify My Calculations to compute your percent differences: \[\text{\% Difference} = \frac{\text{Calculated} - \text{Measured}}{\text{Measured}} \times 100\%\] Then write your 5+ sentence Real-World Reflection in Section 8.

1. What the 6 Graphs Look Like (Slopes Go Down • Areas Go Up)
HORIZONTAL MOTION (Sideways — Constant Speed)
1. Horizontal Position \(x(t)\) Shape: Straight sloped line starting at \(x_0\).
Meaning: Ball moves same meters every second.
2. Horizontal Velocity \(v_x(t)\) Shape: Flat horizontal line (\(v_x = m\)).
Rule: Above \(0\) if going right; below \(0\) if going left!
3. Horizontal Accel. \(a_x(t)\) Shape: Flat line right on 0 (\(a_x = 0\)).
Why: Flat \(v_x\) line has zero slope!
VERTICAL MOTION (Up & Down — Gravity \(a_y \approx -9.8\text{ m/s}^2\))
1. Vertical Position \(y(t)\) Shape: Downward arch (parabola).
Points: Start \(y_0\) → Peak \(y_{\max}\) → End \(y_{\text{final}}\).
2. Vertical Velocity \(v_y(t)\) Shape: Straight line sloping down.
Crosses 0 at \(t_{\text{apex}}\)! Slope = \(a_y\).
3. Vertical Accel. \(a_y(t)\) Shape: Flat line below \(0\) (\(\approx -9.8\text{ m/s}^2\)).
Why: Gravity stays constant the whole flight!

2. How to Read & Sketch Motion Graphs (From a \(v_x\) and \(v_y\) Graph)

A. Find \(a_y\) from \(v_y\) (Use SLOPE)

Pick any 2 points \((t_1, v_{y1})\) and \((t_2, v_{y2})\) on the \(v_y\) line:

\(a_y = \text{Slope} = \dfrac{v_{y2} - v_{y1}}{t_2 - t_1}\)
  • Slope goes downhill → negative!
  • Label units: m/s\(^2\).
  • Same number on \(a_y(t)\) flat line!

B. Find Rise, Drop & Height (Use TRIANGLE AREA)

\(\text{Triangle Area} = \tfrac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height}\)
  1. Rise (+): Triangle above \(t\)-axis (\(0\) to \(t_{\text{apex}}\)): \(\Delta y_{\text{up}} = +\tfrac{1}{2} \cdot t_{\text{apex}} \cdot v_{0y}\)
  2. Peak Height: \(y_{\max} = y_0 + \Delta y_{\text{up}}\) (Add start \(y_0\)!)
  3. Drop (−): Triangle below \(t\)-axis (\(t_{\text{apex}}\) to \(t_{\text{end}}\)): \(\Delta y_{\text{down}} = -\tfrac{1}{2} \cdot (t_{\text{end}} - t_{\text{apex}}) \cdot |v_{y,\text{end}}|\)
  4. Net Change: \(\Delta y_{\text{net}} = \Delta y_{\text{up}} + \Delta y_{\text{down}}\)
  5. Final Height (e.g. rim): \(y_{\text{final}} = y_0 + \Delta y_{\text{net}}\)

C. Sketch \(x(t)\) & \(y(t)\) With Numbers

  • Horizontal \(x(t)\):
    1. Find \(\Delta x = v_x \cdot t_{\text{end}}\) (rectangle area).
    2. Plot Start \((0, x_0)\) & End \((t_{\text{end}}, x_0 + \Delta x)\).
    3. Connect with a straight line.
  • Vertical \(y(t)\):
    1. Plot 3 dots: Start \((0, y_0)\), Peak \((t_{\text{apex}}, y_{\max})\), End \((t_{\text{end}}, y_{\text{final}})\).
    2. Connect with a smooth arch.
  • Put evenly spaced numbers on axes!
3. Four Fast Concept Questions (Copy These Key Ideas!)
1. When is the ball at the TOP? At the exact time where the \(v_y\) line crosses \(0\) (\(v_y = 0\)).
2. Going UP vs. DOWN? Up: where \(v_y > 0\) (above axis).
Down: where \(v_y < 0\) (below axis).
3. At rest at the top? NO! Vertical \(v_y = 0\), but horizontal \(v_x \neq 0\) (still moving sideways).
4. Why is \(a_y < 0\) everywhere? Gravity always pulls down: slows ball going up, speeds ball going down.
